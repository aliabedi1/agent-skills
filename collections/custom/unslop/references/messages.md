# Message rules

Overlay for messages sent to a named recipient: email, Slack, chat. The writing base rules apply; this file holds only the deltas specific to messages.

A message is not a short document. Most of what makes a document good, sections, signposting, a closing summary, makes a message worse.

## Ban list

**Openers**
- `Hope this finds you well`, `Just wanted to reach out`, `I wanted to touch base`, `Circling back on this`, `Per my last email`: filler that delays the first real sentence, open with the actual content

**Sign-offs**
- `Warmly`, `my door is always open`, `Please don't hesitate to reach out`, `Looking forward to hearing your thoughts`: pick a plain sign-off, or none, and stop when the content stops

## Rules

### Send the note, don't compose the document
Prevent: Size the message to the fact it carries. When the whole message is one or two facts, write one or two sentences and stop, with no framing, no context paragraph, and no closing offer of further help. A message under roughly 100 words needs no shaping beyond saying the thing clearly.
Detect: Find short messages padded with an opening that announces the subject, a paragraph explaining background the recipient already has, or a closing line offering help nobody asked for, and cut back to the sentences carrying actual information.

### No document furniture in a message
Prevent: Write a message as paragraphs. No headings, no bolded section labels, no bolded opening sentences standing in for headings, and no numbered list unless the items are genuinely a sequence or a count the reader needs. If a message has enough separate moves to want sections, that is a sign it should be a document, with a two-line message pointing at it.
Detect: Find headings, bolded lead-ins, and section labels inside a message and flatten them into paragraphs or a plain list, and where the structure is genuinely load-bearing, move the content into a document and replace the message with a short pointer to it.

### Greeting and sign-off match the relationship
Prevent: Open and close the way the actual relationship and medium call for. A line acknowledging something shared and recent, a meeting last week, a talk you saw, is content, not throat-clearing, and belongs in a message to someone you know. Chat messages often need no greeting at all. Sign off with a name, or a plain word and a name.
Detect: Find greetings and sign-offs that do not match the relationship, an effusive opener to a stranger, a formal close to a colleague, an adverb-plus-name sign-off, and replace each with what this specific relationship supports, without stripping a genuine acknowledgement that carries information.

### Don't tell the recipient what you know about them
Prevent: Facts about the recipient from your own notes or briefing, their role, whether they are the only attendee, what they are worried about, are context for deciding what to write. Use them to shape the message; do not state them back.
Detect: Find sentences that report the recipient's own situation to them, and cut each, keeping whatever decision it shaped.
